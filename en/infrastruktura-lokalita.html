Puerto Jiménez, your gateway to Osa

  • A small harbour town
    On the shore of the Golfo Dulce, luxury and untouched nature become one. Discover a safe retreat for living, and an investment with no real equivalent elsewhere.
  • One of four tropical fjords in the world
    The Golfo Dulce is a strictly protected place found nowhere else. It is the only spot on the planet where whales from both hemispheres meet and give birth. That rarity permanently underpins how exclusive the investment is.
  • Corcovado National Park within reach
    Live on the threshold of the most biologically rich place on the planet. Endless jungle, full of exotic wildlife, begins just beyond the gates of your protected estate. Legal protection of the park also prevents the surroundings from being built over.
  • An ocean full of things to do
    From surfing world-famous waves and snorkelling on coral reefs to watching dolphins and whales from a boat. Puerto Jiménez is a year-round paradise.
  • A settled community, with a Czech team behind you
    You do not have to be there on your own. You are making a home in a safe international community, with the full support of a Czech team on the ground, who look after management and letting.
  • A morning ritual in some of the cleanest nature on earth
    You wake in untouched nature with a cup of Costa Rica’s famous coffee. You pick exotic fruit beside your villa, and the only ones you share it with are the local monkeys and parrots.

When feeling meets good sense

The Osa Peninsula is one of the last large untouched parts of Costa Rica. The state keeps it as a biological corridor and almost never allows new building.

The wild beauty of Corcovado National Park, the remarkable tropical fjord of the Golfo Dulce and the world-famous surf stay within reach of Puerto Jiménez.

People who want to live here buy land in a guarded estate and build a villa with the Czech team that has been on the ground since 2016.

The map shows the existing international airports in San José and Liberia, which are how people reach the Osa Peninsula today.

Limits on building

On the Osa Peninsula they are among the strictest in Latin America. The state is pursuing a strategy that keeps the peninsula as a biological corridor, and stops a repeat of what happened in other parts of Costa Rica, where unchecked building destroyed coastal ecosystems.

Growth in tourism

Forget beaches lined with sun loungers and souvenir sellers. The beaches on Osa are wild, lined with coconut palms and thick jungle that comes almost down to the surf. It is common to have a beach several kilometres long entirely to yourself. Your only company will be the crabs, and the parrots flying overhead.

Pura vida, within reach

Costa Rica is, above all, a way of life. Our clients buy a villa they are glad to come back to, and when they are away the team on the ground can manage it and let it. The Osa Peninsula is one of the last, and most strictly protected, untouched places in Central America.
